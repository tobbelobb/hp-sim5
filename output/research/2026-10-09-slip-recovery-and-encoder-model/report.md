# Recovering autocal slips and retaining fixed encoder errors

This investigation implements bounded reduced-force recovery after a fixed encoder slips and automatic closed-loop/layering/recording startup. An isolated production JS/RRF fault injection caused 8.01-degree fixed B drift. The collector restored position at lower commands and completed both directions, retaining 20 valid points with B span 0.47 degrees and maximum fixed error 0.38 degrees. A closed-loop bootstrap with automatic travel measurement completed 60 points. A real recorder connection preserved its session and acknowledged initial physics before G-code events. Trial-005 fixed encoders span at most 0.01 degrees; its final rank-zero fit had no valid next candidate. Finite-residual comparisons show that current objectives ignore per-point fixed encoder variation: perturbing raw fixed observations by ±0.7 degrees, or reversing fixed-angle associations while preserving modeled medians, leaves residuals identical. The recommended extension uses measured per-point fixed lengths and propagated uncertainty in both NumPy/JAX pointwise objectives and held-out validation. That objective extension is proposed, not implemented. No hardware recovery or improved calibration accuracy is established.

## Collection behavior

Previously a fixed encoder exceeding 1.5 degrees aborted the collection. Now a typed finite-drift error requests position hold for every motor, halves the free-motor mid/maximum commands and any explicit sensor measurement command, preserves idle preload and never increases a smaller explicit command. It returns the motors to encoder origin one at a time, repositions the fixed targets, and recollects the entire affected direction. Up to three retries are permitted. Reduced settings carry into subsequent directions/sweeps and the saved force configuration. Transport/invalid-data errors are not treated as force failures, and exhausted recovery holds before aborting.

This deliberately recollects a direction rather than mixing earlier points with changed measurement forces. The final dataset contains only successful attempts; raw extended events/partial journals preserve prior observations with attempt markers and `sweep_recovery` events. Each point records fixed target angles, raw and averaged fixed errors, and actual commanded sensor force; direction metadata retains sensor and pullout commands. The original trial recordings remain unchanged.

The first final-policy test injected open-loop behavior only in its owned physics service, allowing actual modeled slipping despite autocal requesting closed loop. It used recorded HP3 scene/layering settings and the production RRF bridge, command controller, encoder conversion and collector. The verified fault produced8.01 degrees of B drift with 6.44246 N commanded sensor force. One retry changed sensor force to 3.22123 N and pullout to 2.27272 N. It restored targets and collected 20 points in two directions, with B span 0.47 degrees, D span 0.37 degrees and maximum raw/mean fixed error 0.38 degrees. No force sensor input was used. [Dataset](overload-guard-recovery-verified/sweeps.json), [collector log](overload-guard-recovery-verified/collector.log), [physics](overload-guard-recovery-verified/physics.jsonl), [boundary events](overload-guard-recovery-verified/events.jsonl).

An earlier probe recorded the correct physical behavior but inaccurate pullout metadata because the explicit sensor override was inadvertently passed to its metadata resolver. The verified probe fixes this. Preserve [earlier dataset](overload-guard-recovery/sweeps.json) as diagnosis evidence rather than treating its pullout metadata as authoritative.

## Simulator startup

Before movement, autocal requests closed loop and acknowledges the returned settings. Nonzero explicit buildup, firmware M666 Q, or fitted modeled buildup enables line layering. The browser uses its actual feature controller so toggles and resources agree. A layering change rebakes/resets initial cable lengths; speed/trace are reapplied afterward. A stale browser missing the settings acknowledgement fails with a reload instruction.

The simulator closed-loop implementation projects position-controlled spools directly to target angles. It is an ideal constraint, not a torque-limited hardware controller. An encoder alone does not imply that feedback is enabled or that a physical motor has enough torque to recover. Free force-controlled spools remain on their force dynamics path. Therefore closed loop can remove fixed encoder slip without guaranteeing a smooth effector trace or a valid calibration.

Automatic travel measurement now uses the capped pullout command rather than an unusually high tuning edge command. The production closed-loop bootstrap completed all three configurations/six directions and 60 points: raw travel 539.977 mm, scaled 431.982 mm, 1677.844 simulated seconds in 96.98 wall seconds. Smaller force changes the measured travel/coverage; no full optimization accuracy comparison was run. [Bootstrap dataset](patched-bootstrap-auto-size/sweeps.json), [log](patched-bootstrap-auto-size/collector.log), [clock](patched-bootstrap-auto-size/clock.json).

`--extended-reference-ws` requests a browser recorder connection and extended events; existing sessions at the same address stay connected. Collection waits for acknowledgement of its initial physics sample, not just an open socket. Python/collector startup metadata is allowed before the browser connects; G-code events still require a connected physics source. Failed connection clears backpressure and fails the requested extended capture clearly. A separate active recording at a different address is preserved and reported as a conflict.

The [real connection test](recording-startup.mjs) runs the existing Python receiver and browser FlightRecorder in Node without a renderer. It saved one RRD with three physics samples and four events, no rejected messages, stride 10, applied settings true and no pending samples. It verified that a second request preserved recording session identity. [Result](recording-startup/result.json), [receiver output](recording-startup/receiver.log). The first harness attempt used the logger's wrong argument type, failed before browser connection, and left an empty recording, preserved beside the successful recording. This is not a browser UI execution; resource/UI integration is tested separately.

## Optimization audit and recommendation

The collection guard deals with large loss of position. For smaller deviations, the measured encoder angle should define actual geometry rather than the command target. A cogging-induced physical displacement is not itself an unknown encoder measurement error. Avoid forcing exact zeros or assuming a commanded force equals every branch tension.

Current code uses raw drive/sensor angles for modeled lengths, drive/sensor means and noise for residuals, aggregate friction/cogging/flex noise terms, and fixed-angle medians for one fixed length per sweep. It does not preserve fixed angles' association with individual points in the objective, and fixed-axis noise is not propagated through geometry. The JAX packing likewise stores fixed deltas per sweep, not per point.

E3 compares finite residual arrays at true simulator anchors, with filtering/flex disabled so no discarded observations or fitted corrections can hide sensitivity. Adding+/-0.7 degrees to trial-005 raw fixed observations leaves the raw-length residuals exactly identical. Reversing fixed encoder observations in trial-004 while preserving their distribution leaves spool-transformed residuals exactly identical. All residual arrays are asserted finite and present; this is not an equality of empty or invalid outputs. [Audit script](encoder-objective-audit.py), [results](encoder-objective-audit.json).

The recommended first change is a candidate-dependent per-point geometry model, not an unconstrained data-cleaning step:

1. In `dataset_with_modeled_lengths`, retain each point's fixed lengths using its measured angles and the candidate spool transform/reference. Preserve raw data.
2. In `_pointwise_sweep_residuals` and the matching JAX path, predict geometry for those fixed lengths per observation. Keep the shared-ellipse prefit as an approximate initializer, not the final model when fixed lengths vary.
3. Propagate fixed encoder uncertainty into residual variance using geometry derivatives at the candidate anchors. Small repeatability sigma does not estimate systematic bias; retain physical floors or bounded priors.
4. Use the same model for `_data_cost`, final fitting, frozen held-out predictions and their relevant derivatives/information diagnostics. Otherwise history ranking could compare incompatible models.

Only after this, consider bounded encoder-zero/cogging nuisance parameters with priors. Free per-point offsets or projecting observations onto a fitted ellipse can absorb wrong anchor geometry, lower costs without improving calibration and invalidate uncertainty. Test synthetic fixed-angle deviations with known anchors, NumPy/JAX values/gradient parity and held-out prediction before changing scores or default optimization. This extension is documented in both requested overviews and remains proposed.

## Trial-005 and limits

Trial-005 has 11 sweeps/220 points. Fixed encoders are constant to.01 degree. Its final iteration 9 selects an underconstrained sentinel with cost 100, rank 0/12 and D_z=0, exhausts recovery, and prints `No valid candidate to collect; stopping.` That path does not produce the normal final acceptance verdict. Thus fixed-motor slip does not explain this recorded failure. The PjRt warning internals and optimization degeneracy are not fixed or attributed to a root cause here. [Recorded log](../../extended-autocal/trial-005/sweeps.full_auto.log), [dataset](../../extended-autocal/trial-005/sweeps.json), [manifest](../../extended-autocal/trial-005/hangprinter-20261009T112534.990912Z-def17037.json).

Validation: 23 JS suites/126 tests passed, followed by a focused 4-test recovery rerun with one added floor regression; 17 Python tests pass for recorder contents/lifecycle, stage replay and learned layering. Live experiments use own local ports/services and 300-second collector subprocess deadlines. No user browser, hardware or source reference data was modified. Real hardware recovery, full browser UI capture and improved numerical calibration accuracy remain untested.
