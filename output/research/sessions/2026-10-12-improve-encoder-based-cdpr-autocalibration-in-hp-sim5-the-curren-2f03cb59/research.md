Initial first-principles ideas (not yet tested):
1. Exploit redundant cable length consistency with latent poses (bundle adjustment / Schur elimination), instead of fitting a short arc's ellipse summary. Known radii remove scale ambiguity; fix rigid-motion gauge explicitly.
2. Evaluate residual geometry at actual per-point fixed encoder lengths. A fixed motor's measured drift is observed geometry, not independent noise; ignoring it can systematically move anchors.
3. Propagate uncertainty from all encoders through constraint sensitivities, retain a systematic error floor, and avoid weighting only by near-zero quiet-sample variance.
4. Select/stop using predictions on a common independent sweep set, not incomparable future sets or training-score iteration penalties. Test disagreement with known geometry; never select using truth in production.
5. Fewer spread-out points on wider arcs, or optimal design based on task-space pose uncertainty. Redundant samples on a tiny arc add little observability.
6. Continuous low-speed encoder trajectories with dynamic transient rejection, rather than settle at every point. Requires a new measurement contract and independent dynamics tests.
7. Profile pullout, force tuning, repeated settle waits, and solver/planner separately. Remove redundant waiting only when the same settling certificate remains valid.
8. Warm starts / shared compiled geometry / analytic elimination to avoid repeated optimization and planning work.

Literature: encoder-only redundant constraints are used by Yuan et al., Applied Sciences 2019, DOI 10.3390/app9112182 (publisher abstract/full text search result). Caverly et al. 2025, DOI 10.1115/1.4065236, propose covariance-derived position/orientation precision metrics for selecting points and stopping; institution abstract inspected. These motivate hypotheses, not evidence of HP3 improvement.
