# Autocal research allocation after mixed consensus evidence

Status: complete (desk exercise; next numerical assignment not tested).

## Abstract

What should receive the next research allocation after broader replay rejects
blanket finite-prediction consensus membership? This desk exercise compares saved
consensus, ranking, solver-attribution and model-adequacy evidence with current
selection code. CM-E1 reports one gain and two regressions across eight additional
datasets; all three changed comparisons lack common unseen sweeps. The agenda
therefore retains production defaults and proposes a bounded compatible-sweep
search, with at most six later frozen prediction evaluations if eligible data
exist. It compares four routes and demonstrates how withdrawing CM-E1 would
replace that allocation with evidence recovery while preserving other findings.
Direct claim links suffice; no graph infrastructure was added. Saved results
were inspected, not independently reproduced. Some historical raw artifacts are
unavailable here. No fits, selection/prediction experiments, physics, collection
or hardware measurements were performed; improved calibration accuracy is not
established by this workflow result.

## AG-D1: one allocation from scoped evidence

The [agenda](../../../../research/autocal-agenda.md) retains the existing human
goal, compares four routes and makes one allocation. CM-E1 supplies a negative
nonworsening result, not a negative result for every form of consensus. Historical
ranking reports disagree in their net direction but share mixed per-dataset
outcomes and different-fold limitations. Their repeated fixture names and
different composite metrics prevent treating them as independent confirmation
or aggregating them with CM-E1's anchor-only sum.

Current `select_history_plan` ranks individual candidates, then applies consensus
from all scored candidates while retaining the chosen spool model. Its reported
single-fit score is not the applied average's score. `stopping.py` independently
consumes raw-history consensus; a shared-helper change would require trajectory
evidence. These source boundaries match the prior integration and CM-E1 record.
The [saved coverage](../2026-10-23-consensus-membership/results.json) leaves
`flexible_lines_2000`, `ten_points_w_buildup` and `ten_points_w_buildup2` without
a common unseen fold. This supports a later compatible-data check before another
rule or threshold search. It does not establish that extra saved data exist.

Alternatives remain consequential: model bias/identifiability can explain good
prediction with poor geometry; robust fitting can fail before selection; better
collection may be needed eventually. Lane 02's individual/final attribution
correction removes a specific rollback rationale. Lane 01's nominal truth audit
blocks redefining truth to a fitted reference. The fixed-encoder model report
supports a separately scoped sensitivity route, while its low-slip failure
prevents declaring slip the sole cause. These are linked scoped findings, not
proof of a new algorithm or hardware behavior.

AG-D1 is supported as a workflow exercise: a next researcher can reach exact
inputs, source boundaries, rival explanations, decisions and stop conditions
from one ordinary Markdown note. No lookup code was needed. The proposed later
probe is limited to three base histories, twelve candidate files, six prediction
evaluations and 30 minutes; all fresh fits, physics and collection remain zero.
