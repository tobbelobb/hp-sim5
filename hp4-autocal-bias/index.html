Why HP4 can measure the wrong cable length

In the HP4 simulation, moving the printer head to the side makes it tilt a tiny amount. One of two paired lower cables then loses its pull. A motor encoder still turns, but the calibration software interprets that movement as though both cables were pulling. The result: it estimates the wrong anchor positions.

01 / Where the problem begins

One upper line ends 9 mm sideways

HP4 has three D lines at the top, all driven by one spool. In the original simulation, each line ends just past a pair of wheels. The offset is small, but the three endings pull in a slightly unbalanced pattern.

The green point is the new simulated termination. The orange point is the original. The diagram is a simplified view along the wheel axle; exact 3D coordinates appear below.

02 / What the upper lines do

The printer head rolls when it moves sideways

Roll means a small tilt of the moving platform. In the original HP4 replay, it changes by about 0.21° for every metre of sideways travel. With the D attachment moved, the same type of replay gives 0.008°/m.

The tilt in the drawing is deliberately exaggerated. The measured values come from simulation replay, not a physical HP4.

03 / Why that matters for measurement

One of the paired lower cables stops pulling

Each A, B or C spool controls two parallel cable paths. When the platform rolls, one path may go nearly slack (very little force). The other stays taut (carrying the load). In the replay, roughly half of the measurement poses contain a strand below 0.1 N.

A spool encoder measures rotation, not the tension of both branches. Its reading no longer has the simple length meaning expected by the calibration model.

01 · D-line routing and attachment position

The top-line layout that creates the problem

HP4 has three upper D lines driven from one spool. In the simulation, each D line passes four wheels before ending at the ceiling unit: SpoolD → WheelD0_left_top → WheelD0_left_bottom → WheelD0_right_top → WheelD0_right_bottom → AttachD0. The upper wheel centers are 6 mm apart. In the original scene, the final attachment point sits another 3 mm beyond the second wheel along their axle. That creates a total sideways offset of 9 mm from the first wheel position to the attachment point.

One D line, viewed from the side

Follow the continuous cable in four steps. Blue goes down, orange crosses up, purple goes down again, and red or green returns to the knot at the ceiling. The spool feed is omitted. The dashed line is the intended center between the two wheels.

Side projection along the wheel axle; horizontal distances greatly enlarged relative to the 1.9 m vertical span. The knot also sits 6.5 mm inward, matching the inward tangent of the bearing. That intentional alignment is invisible in this view. This is a wheel-center diagram, not the pulley contact path.

The 6.5 mm inward offset is intentional

At the origin, the upper and lower bearing centers are directly above one another. But the cable touches the rims of the bearings, 6.5 mm from their centers. The knot must line up with the inward tangent of the first bearing, exactly one pulley radius inward. Moving it inward or outward would disturb the radial balance.

Look down on one D path. Each bearing appears as a narrow rectangle because it is viewed edge-on. Blue rings show the four lower contacts, and colored dots show the upper contacts. The diamond is the fixed knot, which counts as the fourth upper contact.

Now look down on all three D paths

The same offset repeats around the equilateral triangle, rotated 120° at each corner. That is the handedness that drives the unwanted roll. After the change, each upper effective center sits above its lower counterpart.

Moving each attachment 9 mm along the axle removes the transverse lever arm of this path. Reported rough-bootstrap replay roll falls from 0.21°/m to 0.008°/m for HP4, and to 0.003°/m for reduced HP5. This does not establish that the hardware CAD has already been changed.

Hardware question, not yet resolved: Is the real HP4 D termination offset in the same tangential direction as the original simulated scene? Inspect the knot position in the local axle direction of each D path, not only pulley-center spacing. The manual renderings show how to route strands, but do not by themselves measure the as-built termination coordinates.

03 · Exact named cable paths

Which exact lines are involved?

Upper D (or I) set

Three D paths (HP4), or four I paths (pulley-reduced HP5), share a single upper spool. Their lateral wheel-to-termination geometry determines how much body rotation is required to equalise lengths during translation. The branch correction changes the last attachment point, not the wheel centres or the total number of reeving paths.

Lower ABC loops

For each of A, B and C, the L and R lines share one motor and each follows a pair of free-running effector wheels. The left/right strands can carry very different loads when the effector rolls. A point model assuming both remain tensioned is then incorrect.

04 / Why the encoder stops meaning what autocal thinks it means

What it means for one ABC strand to go slack

An ABC spool controls two parallel runs, each routed through free effector wheels. When one becomes almost tensionless, the spool encoder no longer represents the equal-length average assumed by a point-based measurement model.

The branch that disappears from the model

Topology illustration

Nearly slack: the right strand no longer supplies an equivalent length constraint. The curved line is schematic, not the computed slack shape.

Observed in the replay

One encoder, two possible load paths

In approximately half of the 256 recorded HP4 measurement poses, at least one member of an ABC pair was below 0.1 N. Modeling the observed taut branch explained the encoded lengths to a reported 0.012 mm.

This makes a low residual particularly misleading: the wrong constraints can be internally very consistent.

A point fit can be very precise but systematically wrong when a paired line's active load path changes between measurement poses. A spool encoder measures total reel motion; it is not an independent tension sensor for each strand.

One-line intervention: increase force per effective strand

In autocal/equal_strain.py, the target changes from 5 × start force / min(N·U) to 5 × start force / min(N). For reeved U = 2 configurations, this doubles the measurement strand force target. HP3 and direct HP5 have U = 1 and are unaffected by the formula change.

05 · Remaining systematic error

Why the remaining error depends on where “zero” is measured

Higher collection tension prevents strand unloading and improves the recovered shape of the anchors. It also increases the length difference between the slack or lightly loaded zeroing state and the tensioned measurement state. Near-horizontal ABC lines convert this length offset into apparent height. Raising tension further does not solve that translation ambiguity.

Design implication: establish or re-measure encoder zero at the same tension used for calibration, ideally with the effector held at the known home pose. This protocol is proposed by the researcher; it has not yet been demonstrated on real hardware and must be checked for safe forces, friction, and repeatability.

06 · Requirements for hardware designers

What hardware designers should check on the real machine

  1. Document upper termination geometry. Specify the wheel centers, crossing strand and termination point in each D/I path in a local tangential/radial frame. Check all three (HP4) or four (HP5 reduced) rotated copies; a common spool means small asymmetries couple.
  2. Make the upper line pivot nearly non-chiral. Simulate or measure effector roll over the intended horizontal workspace, not only at the home pose. The corrected scene's 0.008°/m (HP4) and 0.003°/m (HP5 reduced) are reference results, not universal acceptance tolerances.
  3. Maintain load in both strands of each lower pair during calibration. Measure or estimate strand tension separately and inspect the minimum across the calibration region. The simulated 0.99 N target permits widespread unloading; 1.98 N is effective in the tested HP4 scene, but actual force limits need physical validation.
  4. Match home and measurement tension. A low-load encoder origin followed by tensioned sweeps produces common length and height offsets even if the anchor shape is correct. Test tensioned homing before trying to fix it with increasingly elaborate stiffness parameters.
  5. Validate against ground truth beyond fit residuals. Test roll, per-strand tension, final anchor shape and common translation independently. Good fit precision does not prove accuracy when the active cable set switches.

Status of evidence: The causal mechanism is strong for the replayed simulator revisions. Whether an assembled HP4 exhibits the same mirror-asymmetric knot placement remains unverified; the manual images describe routing but not an as-built 3D measurement. The new scene correction is committed on the research branch but hardware CAD and the proposed tensioned home-reference procedure are separate tasks.
