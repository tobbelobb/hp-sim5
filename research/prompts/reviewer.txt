Take a critical look at the newest research report, and the associated code, ie the latest commit.

 - Is the experiment(s) informative?
 - Is the result reproducible?
 - Did the algorithm actually improve something important?
 - Did we change multiple variables simultaneously?
 - Is a surprising result a discovery or a bug?
 - Is the delivered code in line with the repo's overarching goals?
 - Does the delivered features overviewable and maintainable?

Grade the most recent work along a scale:
 Grade 1: The whole recent work has obvious fundamental flaws and the commit should be discarded. The report is mostly just valuable just as a documented example on how a research process can derail into non-relevance.
 Grade 2: Parts of it is flawed and should be discarded. The remaining core or branch can be regarded as promising and deserves further exploration.
 Grade 3: The work is promising but incomplete and must be continued, or discarded if continuing it is deemed too expensive.
 Grade 4: The work shows substantial promise, and even some proven value.
 Grade 5: The work is fantastic and even innovative in how it manages to add rigour and value. An example to others to learn from.

Suggest three ways to improve quality of the parts that we should keep.
