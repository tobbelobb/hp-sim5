# Autocal after the DaRUS spool changes

**Recommendation.** Keep today's Hangprinter benchmarks as controls. Add a paired winding-history experiment before changing the fitter. The decisive question is whether returning to the same pose, encoder angle and current tension by two winding histories changes the measured range enough to bias an anchor. Start on HP3, then repeat on HP5 pulley-reduced to test the tension and reeving assumptions.

## What actually changed between b30dd333 and b56301d2

The production Rust solver can store geometric drum occupancy separately from unstretched material. On winding, it saves how much material corresponds to the drum rotation at the winding tension. On payout it releases the most recently wound material first. The per-end stack is bounded and coalesced. The running radius can also contract with tension. Nominal radius and geometric buildup already existed. Free-span elasticity, retained elongation and stick-slip also predate this comparison. [Spool model and validation](../../../ai_docs/externally_captured_reference_data/DATA_CARD.md) · [Rust equations](../../../src/wasm/cable_solver/src/engine.rs)

At the two revisions compared below, both effects were opt-in. The default was zero radial contraction and disabled strained storage. COPacabana enabled strained storage on eight paths, initializing them from the P0 force readings. It initially left radial contraction at zero. The four Hangprinter CI scenes and the two generated hysteresis/stick-slip variants enabled neither new effect. Geometric line layering has its own switch. The diff contains no autocal estimator, collector or benchmark workflow changes. [Scene attributes](../../../public/usd_scenes/copacabana.usda) · [Path and world defaults](../../../src/js/cable_joints_3d/rust_physics_engine.js) · [Benchmark scenarios](../../../autocal/tools/benchmark_history.py)

The subsequent production change enables strained storage and radius-scaled radial flattening by default on every elastic spool, in hp-sim-3d, headless autocal and the Rust API. When reproducing the historical control on a newer build, explicitly disable both effects.

## First experiment for the next agent

1. **Separate span elasticity from spool conversion.** On a target rope and drum, measure a known span at two loads with the drum locked. Then wind a known length at low and high tension and pay it out against each of those same two loads. Use a calibrated load cell and independent travel or position measurement. Repeat with the same starting layer and with the order reversed. A settled, matched-tension pass probes the change in running radius. Changing only the winding tension at the same payout tension probes the stored history. Static dwell at fixed drum angle helps distinguish creep. Reproduce this schedule in the simulator with frozen motor inputs before running the adaptive collector.

2. **Use the two tensions explicitly.** For a nearly constant geometric radius `R`, the model predicts paid-out stretched length per nominal drum arc of approximately `(1 − k_r F_wind/R) × (1 + F_out/EA)/(1 + F_wind/EA)`, away from the radius clamp. The strain terms cancel at equal tension. At fixed winding tension, changing payout tension tests span strain. Use strand tension and the actual reeving when translating a motor torque or an axis force. The 6 mm D-Pro identification on a grooved industrial drum is evidence for this mechanism. Thin, multilayer Hangprinter line needs its own coefficients and uncertainty. [Rig results by rope](../../../ai_docs/externally_captured_reference_data/DATA_CARD.md)

3. **Make the history observable before fitting it.** Preserve ordered encoder increments, home/reference events, time, per-motor force requests and the source of each realized-force estimate. Cover travel between samples, including homing, pullout, reversals and return-to-origin. Record initial fill and the preload used to lay the starting coils. For uncertain factory winding, either precondition the section the test will pay out or label that section as unknown. Returning an encoder to zero can leave different material on the drum. A simulator checkpoint can carry the stack across a resume. A new USDA scene or a flight-recorder observation only supplies initial settings or observations. Store simulator truth separately for diagnosis. Assess the deployed estimator with signals the machine can actually measure.

4. **Start the estimator with one offline length correction.** Keep the existing firmware radius/buildup conversion for geometric drum travel. Apply a chronological winding/payout correction to recover unstretched material length, then the existing span stretch and reeving correction to predict geometric range. Express the correction relative to the same physical home state. Fit one shared rope/drum coefficient where hardware is shared, with independently constrained EA and radius. Only add parameters if independent measurements distinguish them. Older datasets with insufficient winding history can still use the existing fit. A constant preload and one direction can let a radius error, anchor height, force-scale error and strain compensate one another. A small residual on those data gives little evidence about the anchors. [Spool conversion](../../../autocal/spool_model.py) · [Flex correction](../../../autocal/flex.py) · [Existing identifiability results](../../topics/autocal-stretch.md)

5. **Judge the change on blind geometry and a reversed history.** Pair the same scene, seed, initial state, solver build and controller settings with both effects off, storage only, flattening only and both on. First compare identical motor commands. Then let autocal collect normally. Repeat adaptive runs before attributing a change in sweep count. Keep full sweeps, command history, force estimates, settings and per-line range residuals, binned by direction, previous winding tension and paid-out distance. Report signed anchor-coordinate errors, worst error, held-out pose/range errors, repeat scatter, stopping reason and simulated time. Freeze parameters and reserve an entire winding schedule and a second preload or layer for prediction. Accept a more complex fit only if it improves held-out predictions beyond repeat scatter while preserving the legacy control. CI's durable summary and uploaded logs omit the full sweep dataset and winding history. [CI artifact selection](../../../autocal/tests/test_autocal_benchmark.py)

Use the COPacabana tracker as an independent position observation. Its frame-side force readings have a large static balance residual when interpreted at the platform. Keep those absolute levels out of the calibration objective until the force transfer is independently checked. [PlaneA force audit](report.md#force-channel-audit)

## Firmware and autocal takeaways from DaRUS-3600 / 5171 / 4614 (PR #130)

- **Default solver constants are now in physical cable Newtons (`F_true = √2 · f_logged`).** `DEFAULT_SPOOL_RADIAL_COMPLIANCE` is `0.299e-6 * 0.05018 ≈ 1.50e-8 m²/N` (`E_t ≈ 67 MPa`, giving `0.306 µm/N` on COPacabana's 49 mm drums), and COPacabana's 6 mm D-Pro XTR stiffness is `118.5 kN` across `copacabana.usda`, `RRF/run/vsd/sys/config_copacabana.g` (`M666 S118500.0`), and `public/klipper/copacabana/printer-copacabana-linux-mcu.cfg`. Neither RRF (`M666`), Klipper (`winch`), nor `autocal/spool_model.py` yet models winding-tension history (`F_S`) or drum radial flattening (`k_r`).
- **Separate proximal sensor gain and guide-pulley friction before using actuator forces.** All three Fraunhofer IPA `SW-G3.1` testbeds share a `1/√2` geometric scale factor from the 90° carriage deflection pulley, and COPacabana adds direction-dependent routing friction (`~3.6 %` on 1-pulley lower routing vs `~12.8 %` on 3-pulley upper column routing, plus `56–119 N` per-axis winch/gearbox Coulomb friction). Force-based calibration or firmware flex compensation must not equate winch-side force/torque readings with platform tension.
- **Constant linear `EA` is insufficient at low tensions / heavy payloads.** Linear `EA = 118.5 kN` reproduces only 42–50 % of COPacabana's 13.9 → 29.1 kg vertical sag (`−1.85 mm` simulated vs `−3.69 / −4.40 mm` FARO) and over-predicts `dF` at low-tension poses (`min_F` down to `1–49 N`). Future firmware flex compensation and autocal stretch models should treat low-tension braid softening and structural compliance as first-class hypotheses.
- **Swivel-pulley kinematics and drive lag.** `hp-sim5` models COPacabana's moving swivel pulleys (`SwivelPulleyAPI`, matching `pulley_ik` to `1e-15 m`), whereas RRF (`M669`) and Klipper (`winch`) planners still assume point anchors. DaRUS-4614 also shows a uniform `60.5 ms` CNC/drive tracking lag between `x_tar` and `x`, and `copacabana.usda` motor inertia/damping remain uncalibrated assumptions (bypassed in `planeAEncoders`).
- **Guard against single-trajectory parameter collinearity.** In [calibration_ablation.py](evidence/calibration_ablation.py) (verified `0.084 mm` swivel-pulley IK, trained on PlaneA, tested blind on 7 held-out PlaneA runs and both `CircPath` runs), anchor scales `(sx, sy, sz)`, encoder scale `s_enc`, home offset `d0_z`, and `EA` trade off strongly: fixing `s_enc = 1` (F2) minimizes held-out `CircPath` force (`68.0 N`) while driving `EA` to its `300 kN` bound and hurting PlaneA force (`79.7 N`), while freeing both (F1) reaches `2.74 mm / 38.4 N` on PlaneA and `9.16 mm` on `CircPath` while degrading `CircPath` force to `96.8 N`. Require multi-trajectory or independent survey constraints before accepting fitted geometry changes.
