# Native Rust throughput versus the previous defaults

Status: in progress.

Objective: measure ab833759 native Rust against the exact 7905e64f Wasm headless default, and against the old Python default for fresh research trials.

Hypotheses:
- N01: native improves shared-pipeline throughput across HP3/HP4 logo/autocal and layering on/off.
- N02: continuing-world transport and Rerun reduce the native end-to-end gain.
- N03: fresh research trials gain from replacing their former Python default, including every-step numerical/recording overhead.
- N04: short real firmware collections improve in wall time; simulation steps and settling may differ across math targets.

Constraints: same authored scenes, 2 ms steps, solver settings, real frozen schedules and motor records; no hardware. Default release native build with no RUSTFLAGS changes. Use exact baseline checkout /tmp/hp-sim5-before-native-7905e with the same Node/Python/dependencies. Timing processes are sequential, with no tests/profiles overlapping. Three repetitions for timing families unless an explicitly disclosed diagnostic is used. Preserve all failed tolerance checks and compare finite observations, identity, steps and input hashes. A failed long-run pointwise check is not silently accepted.

Budgets: at most 15 million physics updates, 30 minutes benchmark wall time, 4 GB new artifacts. Live collection, if tested, is isolated, preflighted and bounded to three points in each physical direction, four noise samples, 120 s per job and at most 100,000 steps per job. No optimizer or hardware runs.

Experiments: N01 full eight-case matrix; N02 continuing controlled movement and frozen HP4 collection replay with/without recording; N03 default 200-step fresh trials with/without recording; N04 bounded fresh collection if the preflight and remaining budgets permit. No measured result is available yet.

References: ../../../../research/rust-native-engine.md documents the change; ../2026-10-14-rust-engine/report.md contains the preceding Wasm engine investigation; ../2026-10-13-headless-js-profiling/inputs/origins.json identifies the frozen inputs. Do not reuse old timings as the paired comparison.

N01 comparison policy: explicitly select Wasm at 7905e and native at ab833. Declare atol=rtol=1e-6 before timing. Use the profiler report-only drift option, retaining any failed pointwise check as false; repeated checkpoint hashes within each backend remain exact requirements. This is a performance measurement of fixed schedules, not acceptance of changed trajectories.

N01 complete: all eight exact-baseline/native cases, three repeats per backend (7,544,232 updates). Native throughput ratios: hp3-logo-on=1.208x, hp3-logo-off=1.200x, hp3-autocal-on=1.235x, hp3-autocal-off=1.189x, hp4-logo-on=1.150x, hp4-logo-off=1.161x, hp4-autocal-on=1.151x, hp4-autocal-off=1.145x. All repeat hashes are stable. Three pointwise comparisons pass and five fail the declared 1e-6 mixed-unit tolerance; false flags and error statistics remain in physics/matrix.json. Gains are 14.5–23.5% higher throughput, corresponding to 12.6–19.0% less physics time. Next: consumer observations/recording/fresh defaults and bounded fresh RRF collections.

N02/N03/N04 consumer runner started only after N01 finished. Controlled movement is 1,000 steps (500 A ramp, B torque transition, 499 holds), three repeats for HP3/HP4 with recording on/off. Fresh trials use their actual per-revision defaults, 200 identical steps, recording on/off; include construction and finalized recording in wall time, with simulation-loop time separately recorded. Frozen HP4 consumer replays use a matched 20,000-step prefix for recording comparisons, plus the complete 80,415-step numerical schedule. Live collection uses fresh isolated launcher services, preflight before every collection, unlayered stock HP3/HP4, three points/direction and four noise samples, with 120 s and 100,000-step limits. No autocal optimizer is run. The recorded frozen trace contains observation/motor states but no explicit encoder_response records, so it does not establish parity to original endpoint reads.

N02–N04 timing completed: all controlled/frozen/fresh cases have three repeats per revision. All 24 preflighted six-point firmware collections completed; for each machine they used identical step counts across revisions/repeats (see consumer-matrix.json). Short live collection median gains are only 1.023x HP3 and 1.028x HP4 without recording; recording medians are 0.989x and 0.992x. These small differences require consideration of repeat variability, rather than claiming every local workflow improves. N05 added to cover the standalone autocal headless service and its extended-reference receiver: 10,000 unchanged command steps, HP3/HP4, recording on/off, three repetitions; no optimizer or fresh collection in N05. Then validate current Wasm against exact prior Wasm checkpoint hashes and run detached numerical comparisons outside timing.

N05 completed 24 measured trajectories / 240,000 updates. Including command delivery, encoder query and finalization, no-recorder standalone autocal service gains are 1.179x HP3 and 1.198x HP4. With the production extended-reference recorder, gains fall to 1.062x HP3 and 1.017x HP4. These are fixed motor schedules through the actual Node HTTP/bridge/receiver path, not a complete optimization run. All service step counts are 10,000 and queues drain. N03 supported for default 200-step fresh runs. N04 mixed: 2–3% numerical-mode median gains and about 1% slower recorded medians, with no useful universal end-to-end gain demonstrated.
