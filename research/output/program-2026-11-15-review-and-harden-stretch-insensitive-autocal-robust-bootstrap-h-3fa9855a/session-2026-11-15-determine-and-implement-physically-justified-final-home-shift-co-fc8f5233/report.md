# Final home shift: identifiable only with independent reference evidence

The equal-strain anchor fit fixes three of its home-length offsets to zero as a translation gauge; its remaining offsets are not an absolute home measurement. The existing data do not record the line extensions at the two required reference states, or establish their uncertainty. Neither a fitted offset nor configured stiffness alone identifies physical home. Automatic correction from nominal firmware settings would defeat stretch insensitivity.

The implementation permits a final translation only when the selected fit's dataset provides independently measured effective extensions, their provenance and range uncertainty, and all sweeps were equal strain. It checks 3D geometric rank, a linearized uncertainty limit of 0.5 mm, range consistency within three supplied uncertainties, and a practically slack encoder home. Otherwise it leaves the previous output unchanged and reports the reason. The threshold is an engineering gate, not a statistical guarantee. The extension inputs are in *effective carriage-distance* mm, not spool travel or individual strand extension. No existing collector produces this evidence; ordinary runs therefore do not gain an automatic physical correction.

At the fitted virtual origin let anchor vectors be `a`, fitted gauge offsets `k`, effective line extensions at the measurement reference `e_m`, and extensions at physical encoder home `e_h`. Material-length conservation gives `|a-q| = |a| + k - e_m + e_h`. The correction emits `a-q`. Four affinely independent anchors determine the candidate q from squared-range differences; the original undifferenced ranges check consistency. This assumes the fitted geometry and equal-strain approximation are sufficiently accurate, the extensions refer to the exact fitted reference, and the offsets remain compatible with the selected ellipse fit. Those assumptions have not been verified end to end.

## Firmware reference and limitations

[RRF](../../../../RRF/ReprapFirmware/src/Movement/Kinematics/HangprinterKinematics.cpp) computes geometric length minus origin geometric length **plus** flex, with flex negative under tension. [Klipper](../../../../klipper/klippy/chelper/kin_winch.c) uses the same reference and sign, including mechanical advantage and line count. The flex term is absolute at the origin, not the difference of flex at the current and origin positions. Thus firmware motion already uses a modeled tension-dependent length correction. Raw encoder readings are not necessarily corrected by that fact; any additional correction must account for where the firmware transform was applied and for the actual reference state, rather than blindly adding another nominal flex term. A physical-anchor translation and firmware flex serve different purposes, but an encoder zero established while the line is loaded has an additional material-reference offset that the existing firmware equation does not represent. This implementation rejects such evidence unless its extension is no greater than the stated uncertainty. The offset measurements must include the actual force state, stiffness, guy wire, routing and firmware reference; model-predicted values from an unverified S setting are insufficient.

[Comparison input and script](evidence/comparison.py) and [output](evidence/comparison.json) are deterministic synthetic algebra, not a physics simulation or hardware test. Exact synthetic extensions recover the selected -0.300 mm Z shift to numerical precision. Scaling both extension vectors to 60% (illustrative stiffness mismatch) yields -0.31946 mm, a 0.01946 mm shift error, while the consistency gate accepts it. This deliberately unfavorable example shows that consistency does not establish extension accuracy; correlated wrong inputs can pass. Other geometries and tension states can amplify errors much more. Existing [HP3 study](../../autocal_research_with_Fable5p2/report.md) reported 0.8–2.2 mm home displacement in simulated conditions, not validated hardware accuracy or a measurement of this implementation.

To change the conclusion, acquire independently validated extension and home-reference measurements for the actual collection protocol, quantify their uncertainty and match them to the selected fit, then run repeated held-out physics and hardware comparisons with firmware flex on and off and varied physical versus configured stiffness. No simulator service was started for this algebraic investigation. Tests cover exact sign, absent evidence, inconsistency, loaded home, rank loss and high uncertainty; they do not validate the entire autocal collector-to-firmware path.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, run `.venv/bin/python -m research.output.program-2026-11-15-review-and-harden-stretch-insensitive-autocal-robust-bootstrap-h-3fa9855a.session-2026-11-15-determine-and-implement-physically-justified-final-home-shift-co-fc8f5233.evidence.comparison` and `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q autocal/tests/test_home_compensation.py autocal/tests/test_pose_fit.py autocal/tests/test_stage_replay.py`.

Optional dataset configuration is `home_extension_evidence` with `provenance` (description of independent measurement), `measurement_extension_mm` and `home_extension_mm` (one value per anchor), and positive `range_uncertainty_mm`. The measurement reference is the physical effective extension at the fitted virtual origin under the equal-strain measurement force, not the firmware's commanded force or configured-stiffness estimate. Inputs must come from the same homing and collection reference as the selected fit. The code cannot verify provenance or systematic errors.
