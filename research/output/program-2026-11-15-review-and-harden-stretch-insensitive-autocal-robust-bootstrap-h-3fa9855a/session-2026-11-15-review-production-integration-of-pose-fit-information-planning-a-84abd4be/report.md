# Independent review: production pose fit, planning and equal strain

Reviewed `1254e..5f718` at `5f7189de` on 2026-11-15. The HP3 d2 result in the [preceding report](../../autocal_research_with_Fable5p2/report.md) is a single simulated full-auto result, not a repeatability or hardware claim. This review found a reproducible profile-selection defect and concrete obstacles to extending the new planner to four-high and cube layouts. It does not establish that the defects caused d2's result. No hardware or new physics simulation was run.

## Findings and recommendations

1. **Profile identity drops the fixed targets.** `sensor_force_profiles` generates tables per configuration, but `sensorForceProfileFor` in [sweep_data_collection.mjs](../../../../autocal/control/behaviors/sweep_data_collection.mjs) returns the first entry matching only drive and sensor. A multi-sweep table containing the same pair at different fixed lengths silently selects the wrong force; [lookup.mjs](evidence/lookup.mjs) invokes the actual selector and [output](evidence/lookup-output.txt) shows it. With the HP3 synthetic anchors and fixed A=390 mm, changing fixed D from 0 to −150 mm changes generated sensor force by up to **1.115 N** on the common drive range ([pilot](evidence/pilot.py), [output](evidence/pilot-output.txt)); these are modeled commands, not measured tension errors. Key profiles by sweep identity, including fixed anchors and targets, and direction; reject ambiguous or missing entries. Current three-sweep HP3 bootstrap has distinct axis pairs, and separately generated single-candidate profile files avoid this particular collision.

2. **Projection and spool-fit combinations do not follow the new model.** With `--project-zero-tension`, the collector records extrapolated zero-force readings, yet marks every sweep `sensor_force_profile: true`; `is_equal_strain` uses that flag to trigger automatic home-offset fitting. The projected readings are not observations at the profiled force. Mark the actual measurement model or reject that combination; compare against raw tensioned measurements. Likewise the explicit offsets/spool-fit guard does not cover automatic profile-triggered offsets: the spool branch in [fit_stage.py](../../../../autocal/fit_stage.py) skips the automatic offset fit, while [planning_pass.py](../../../../autocal/planning_pass.py) chooses the pose ranker ahead of the spool-aware ranker. Model and compare these combinations explicitly or reject them until supported. These are code-path findings, not quantified calibration errors.

3. **Force and information models have limited feasibility checks.** The force rule assumes one null direction of a full-rank d-by-(d+1) force matrix and a positive self-stress. The generator clips only sensor force, not other line tensions, and does not establish equilibrium after clipping. Profile extrapolation holds endpoints, and metadata records profile presence rather than achieved strain. Validate all predicted tensions, rank, and actual force range, and report clipping/extrapolation; force and servo tracking need recorded validation. Pose ranking uses fixed 0.5 mm finite differences, a linearized rigid model and a heuristic positive self-stress reachable region. It does not account for measured offsets when constructing candidate fixed-length circles, nor prove that force-driven paths reach them. Validate ranking against held-out observed sweeps and compare designs at equal collection cost. A low residual alone cannot rule out coherent elasticity or poor identifiability.

4. **Pose-fit comparison and offset caution.** `fit_pose_least_squares` performs a fixed 25 Gauss–Newton position steps from home with no convergence or singularity check; robust losses act on individual line residual components after an ordinary least-squares pose, so an outlying line may shift the fitted pose. Multiple starts are compared by `cost` even though each start determines its own robust scale, so their costs are not necessarily comparable objectives. Use a shared scale and evaluate common objective; check convergence and compare against joint or bounded multistart fitting on synthetic outliers. Offset-to-home equivalence is local and the finite prior makes results data and parameterization dependent; retain raw offsets and evaluate absolute as well as translation-aligned errors. These are mathematical review concerns, not a demonstrated failure on d2.

## Broader layouts: synthetic pilot and rollout

The [pilot](evidence/pilot.py) uses ideal symmetric geometries, not scene measurements. For four-high anchors at z=3000 mm and home at the origin, all lines can balance a 1 N downward load with positive tension, yet `predict_sweep_positions` returns zero points: it demands a strictly positive *zero-load* self-stress, impossible when every direction points upward. At home, `equal_strain_tensions` produces mean 0.344 N per strand despite a 1 N requested mean, reflecting an ill-conditioned zero-null weighting; no claim is made that production currently calls these functions for skycam (it does not). Use the affine load equilibrium with nonnegative tensions and actuator limits, test gravity and payload cases, and derive a suitable observable-strain objective rather than applying the HP3 null-vector formula blindly. Four-high also has mirror/height gauge and home-position ambiguity; establish physical plane and gauge constraints and test both branches.

For eight symmetric cube corners, six fixed lengths constrain a one-dimensional sweep only under special dependencies. `_fixed_length_circle` constructs the intersection of the first two spheres and ignores the remaining four: the pilot reports up to **1825.6 mm** error in those four fixed lengths. The existing collector can hold six fixed axes, but compliance and slack make this a different physical problem. A single SVD null vector is also insufficient: 8 lines in 3D have a five-dimensional tension nullspace. Do not enable the current HP3 predictor or force rule for cube corners. Define an active/slack set and constrained equilibrium/compliance model, use all fixed lengths, solve bounded tension allocation, and quantify observability of anchor and offset gauges. Prior [Cube Corners experiments](../../session-2026-10-19-interactive-research-bf5d807a/continuous-evaluation-report.md) observed that six fixed axes can prevent useful motion; this is simulator evidence, not hardware safety evidence.

For both layouts, first add deterministic synthetic geometry and load tests, including infeasible, near-singular and held-out cases; compare information scores with actual estimator error and collector trajectories. Then run bounded recorded headless sweeps with known scene geometry, compare constant force and feasible adaptive force at equal budget, inspect true line forces and travel, and repeat across seeds, compliance and payload. Only after that assess firmware and hardware with appropriate physical limits. The synthetic pilot rejects a direct generalization; a different load-aware design could still work.

## Reproduction and limits

From repository root, run `PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python <investigation>/evidence/pilot.py` and `node <investigation>/evidence/lookup.mjs`; outputs are preserved beside the scripts. The worktree lacked `.venv`, so one was created with system site packages; Python, NumPy and SciPy versions are recorded in [environment.txt](evidence/environment.txt). Existing focused unit tests were also run; see [test output](evidence/tests.txt). The model pilot uses ideal rigid geometry, no noise, no servo dynamics and no real scene configuration. Force lookup magnitude is a difference between two modeled profiles, not a measured error. An end-to-end failure attributable to lookup or the spool/projection branches has not been demonstrated. Counterevidence that would change the conclusions includes a selector keyed by full sweep configuration, a constrained load-aware four-high predictor that finds these poses, and a cube predictor satisfying all six constraints.
