# Stateful two-port pinhole gates

> **Removed, 2026-11-12.** The experimental
> **Hangprinter v4 (pinhole gates, experimental)** machine broke during the
> CableJoints overhaul in `a92b6c45`, and its stateful gate model no longer
> matches the current solver. The overhauled CableJoints solver was also fast
> enough that condensed cable gates were no longer needed for performance, so
> the feature was removed rather than repaired. This note is kept as a record
> of the design and evidence; the scene, generator, code and tests no longer
> exist in the repository.

The removed scene was `public/usd_scenes/hp4_pinhole_gate.usda`, generated by
`scripts/create_hp4_gate_scene.mjs` from the original HP4 scene. Native Rust,
browser Wasm and JavaScript supported the attachment; the independent Python
backend explicitly rejected it.

## Mechanical model

`pinhole_gate` replaces the ABC effector's pinhole-wheel-wheel-pinhole sequence
with six condensed gate units. Each unit has distinct entry and exit ports and
no internal CableJoints. This reduces the HP4 ABC topology from 51 to 33 cable
joints while retaining unilateral cable behaviour on both sides.

The hidden reeving has one material-flow coordinate `q`, and material crosses
the gate only through it. Entry and exit are separate unilateral rows whose
available span is biased by `q`. `q` is a generalized coordinate of both rows
with the reflected bearing inertia as its mass, so it is integrated implicitly
with the cable solve; dry friction is an XPBD friction row on `q`, and viscous
drag acts on its velocity afterwards. The resulting hidden flow law is

```text
Tin - Tout = M qdd + B qdot + Fc sign(qdot)
```

where `M` is the summed bearing inertia reflected through the cable radius, `B`
is viscous drag and `Fc` is dry friction. The friction row holds `q` while the
tension difference stays below `Fc` and opposes slip with `Fc` otherwise; the
cable may carry the flow through zero. This preserves small differences between
entering and exiting CableJoints without recreating the long internal
constraint chain. An earlier explicit update of `q` after the solve became
unstable at the cable's physical stiffness (EA / L with EA = 20 kN gives
ω·dt ≈ 2.2 for `M` above), which is why the gate scene previously divided its
stiffness and damping by six.

The HP4 values are **provisional synthetic values**, not hardware calibration:
`M = 0.023668639053254437 kg`, `B = 0.9467455621301777 Ns/m`,
`Fc = 4e-5 N`, and radius `0.0065 m`. `M` is computed from the two original
bearing inertia tensors; the friction values are placeholders for later
measurement.

Native Rust and Wasm compiled the same law. While the feature existed, the
native/Wasm path ABI was version 3 and its 16-slot path header carried the gate
link, flow, flow velocity, inertia, damping and Coulomb force. Removing the
feature reverted both to ABI 2 and the 9-slot header.

## Visualization

`PinholeGateComponent.visualRoute` / USD `pinholeGate:visualRoute` is a local
polyline independent of the physical constraint solve. The twelve hidden
bearings are rendered as cylinders, not spheres. Their rotation is driven by
actual cable transport through the gate and scaled by
`pinholeGate:rotationScale`.

Replaying the original HP4 shows the top and bottom bearings counter-rotating.
The gate therefore uses top scale `1.0` and bottom scale `-0.338`. The bottom
magnitude is a provisional visual compromise: the original ratio varied by
branch and trial (about `-0.11` to `-0.39` in the 200- and 1000-step probes).
The animation is render-only and does not add physical rotor DOFs.

## Checks

The following checks ran while the feature existed. `npm run test:physics`
rebuilt Native Rust and Wasm and ran the production HP4 Print Logo stability
checks plus loaded-state settling at one, two and four substeps. The flow law
was checked against analytic Atwood results (inertia, dry-friction slip and
static holding) in `src/wasm/cable_solver/tests/cable_physics.rs`. The
gate-specific JS checks (`tests/benchmark/pinhole_gate.test.mjs`, since removed)
covered six gate units, twelve visual cylinders, transport-driven wheel angles
and Native/Wasm agreement.

With the earlier explicit flow law, all 11 physics-gate tests passed. Short HP4 prefix
evidence found a nonzero gate tension difference (maximum `0.0781 N`, mean
absolute `0.0090 N` over 200 steps), while the isolated summed-friction probe
showed breakaway and a `0.0157 N` peak difference. A matched 1000-step native
trial took `146.3 ms` versus `386.0 ms` for the original model (`2.64x`) with a
final-Z difference of `0.000184 m`.

These are short, simulated structural and performance checks. They do not
establish reduced numerical damping, hardware-calibrated realism, long-print
accuracy or full-print success. Browser appearance has not been inspected.
