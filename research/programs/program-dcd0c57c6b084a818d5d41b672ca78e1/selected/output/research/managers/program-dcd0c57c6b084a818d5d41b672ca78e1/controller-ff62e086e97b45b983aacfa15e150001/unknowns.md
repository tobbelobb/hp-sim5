# Important uncertainties and the cheapest useful observation

This is a research allocation map based on inspected repository reports. Proposed rows have not been tested in this program. E1/E2/E3 are complete. A fresh separate reviewer supports their numerical findings within the recorded scope.

| Important uncertainty | Why a decision depends on it | Cheapest discriminator | Status and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What absolute geometry can encoder-only calibration identify when spool conversion is fitted too? | A self-consistent fit could preserve a scale error. More points would not necessarily help. | Scale geometry and spool conversion together on a frozen synthetic control, then hold one measured conversion fixed. Compare finite angle and length residuals. | E2/E3 find a common-scale symmetry when geometry, radii and dimensionful buildup are free. Diverse sweeps leave a local null direction; an assumed independent radius observation removes it. Separately reviewed within this scope. |
| Does the objective use measured fixed-encoder motion? | Collection may retain observations that fitting discards. Extra samples with the same discarded information cannot repair that. | Perturb fixed encoder observations while preserving sweep summaries; compare finite NumPy and JAX residuals, with a drive/sensor perturbation control. | E1 confirms discarded pointwise fixed-angle association in the unfiltered current NumPy/JAX paths, with active aggregate and drive controls. Separately reviewed within this scope. |
| Can the actual applied consensus predict genuinely unseen sweeps? | Different future folds and training overlap can make selection look better without better physical anchors. | Hash training-content unions and freeze one compatible later sweep for both complete applied models. Stop if lineage is missing. | Proposed, not tested here. The existing autocal agenda gives a bounded twelve-file feasibility screen. |
| How much calibration bias comes from cable elasticity, spool winding and a rotating rigid effector? | The point-anchor/ellipse model can fit systematic bias as wrong geometry. | At one fixed pose, repeat a few encoder observations at two preload levels and both approach directions, with independently measured displacement. Change one cause at a time. | Proposed, not tested. Commanded force is not a measured branch tension; a quiet encoder does not establish a stationary effector. |
| Which simulated motion limits survive a timestep and solver-iteration refinement? | Stable default-step motion can have the wrong holding strength or missed-step boundary. | Replay the same frozen short command schedule at 1/2/4/8 substeps, log body pose and encoder lag, and compare against an analytic one-spool control. | Proposed, not tested here. Existing reports already show substep-sensitive Squares tracking and deferred damping/rolling work. A native/Wasm match shares model assumptions. |
| Does calibration accuracy transfer to a real Hangprinter? | Simulation fits and encoder parity cannot validate the physical machine. | Repeat a short closed path to three ruler- or camera-measured Cartesian targets before and after calibration, with a repeat to estimate measurement noise. Keep those target positions out of fitting. | Proposed, not tested. No hardware access in this session. This tests the assembled model and machine, without uniquely identifying which model term is wrong. |

Choose the model sensitivity tests first because they need no new motion and can rule out spending on ineffective extra sweeps. A tape-measured cable displacement per encoder turn is the likely next physical observation if the scale test survives review. It must use actual cable travel at the relevant winding state and account for mechanical advantage.
