# Per-point fixed encoder observations in HP3 calibration

Per-point four-encoder fits did not meet the under-1 mm goal on three prior recordings or in a fresh matched applied comparison. No production change is supported. The idealized test below shows why a single fixed-cable value can be biased, but it does not establish a feasible acquisition policy.

## Model and controlled test

The experiment assumes the four spool radii are known exactly at 30 mm and converts each recorded mean encoder angle to a length difference. For each point, four sphere radii equal the distance from each candidate anchor to the encoder reference origin plus that difference. Subtracting the squared sphere equations solves the position linearly; the residual is the remaining signed range mismatch. Anchor geometry uses the existing Hangprinter parameterization, fixing A x and common lower z at the stock applied value. This is an exploratory unweighted algebraic-trilateration residual, not the production ellipse loss. It can amplify errors in particular geometries and ignores tension, elasticity, correlations and branch ambiguity. No truth was used to select a fit.

[Controlled script](scratchbook/synthetic.py) uses the recorded 7301 encoder readings to construct approximate sample locations, then replaces all ranges with exact geometric ranges from a declared synthetic anchor set. It starts optimization a few millimetres away. With per-point fixed readings the recovered errors A/B/C/D were less than 2e-10 mm; replacing each sweep's fixed-axis ranges by its mean produced errors 2.793/31.454/52.979/34.533 mm and residual RMS 0.323 mm. [Full results](scratchbook/synthetic-results.jsonl). Maximum fixed-axis range across a sweep is 14.709 mm. The constructed points satisfy static geometry by construction, but were not checked for tension, travel or collision feasibility; notably the reconstructed recorded outliers can be far from a physically achievable trajectory. This is a deliberately controlled model discrepancy, not a prediction of actual machine error. The very large fixed-axis variation limits its representativeness.

## Retrospective physical observations

The script fits all points from each prior stock run, starting at its applied firmware geometry. Loss is soft-L1 with transition 0.03, 0.3 or 1000 mm; none was selected using simulated truth. The post-hoc table uses the 0.03 mm noise-scale candidate, roughly corresponding to one 0.06 degree reading at 30 mm. Errors use authored simulated geometry only after fitting, and candidates were never sent to firmware. All coordinates and alternative loss results are retained in [6301](scratchbook/fit6301.json), [7301](scratchbook/fit7301.json), [8301](scratchbook/fit8301.json); [scoring script](scratchbook/score.py).

These are development data previously examined in detail, not independent holdouts. For 7301, raising the soft-L1 transition from 0.03 to 1000 mm reduces residual RMS from 0.819 to 0.702 mm while increasing the largest anchor error from 10.375 to 117.730 mm. Different losses change the geometry drastically; low residual is not an accuracy certificate. The source worlds' baked layouts, unequal friction, readings, firmware and prior failures are documented in the [earlier report](../attempt-c18177d871fb404895e32c03363c8a5e/report.md). No new noise stream or applied M669 was generated here.

It was not used for a physical calibration; the recorded comparisons above had their own documented settings. Sources are at revision `9de0ac2ae01edf2a879c63e42ef269419d349cf0`, with [hashes](scratchbook/source-hashes.txt). Reproduce using the worktree `.venv`, `PYTHONPATH=.` and `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`; scripts take the original run directory. Initial script invocations failed on missing `PYTHONPATH` and a mismatched synthetic perturbation vector; both were corrected before the retained outputs.

Next, test a feasible trajectory with independently verified fixed-axis motion and compare per-point and sweep-average estimators under the same generated noise and force model. A practically useful estimator also needs to handle biased force-dependent ranges and condition-dependent residual scaling, then pass fresh sequential applied trials across layouts and friction settings. No finite trial set proves universal reliability.

## Point-specific ellipse follow-up

For each prior recorded point, I compared its fixed-axis mean encoder length to the scalar recorded at the end of the sweep. The absolute median, 90th percentile, 99th percentile and maximum differences were respectively 0.040/0.092/0.146/0.177 mm for 6301, 0.041/0.097/0.143/0.168 mm for 7301, and 0.027/0.075/0.156/0.180 mm for 8301. [Audit](scratchbook/fixed_audit.py), [6301](scratchbook/fixed-audit6301.json), [7301](scratchbook/fixed-audit7301.json), [8301](scratchbook/fixed-audit8301.json). At 30 mm, 0.06 degree is 0.0314 mm for one reading. A 16-reading independent mean would have 0.00785 mm standard error, but this simple reference ignores temporal correlation and the noise and timing of the sweep scalar; the observed deviations alone do not establish their physical cause. The difference is not automatically a measured cable displacement.

A second estimator retains the production ellipse's approximate-mm residual while assigning each sample its own fixed-circle lengths from the four-axis mean angles. In retrospective development fits, with 0.03 mm soft-L1 transition, errors A/B/C/D were 1.486/1.696/4.428/3.312 mm for 6301, 4.370/3.990/1.776/3.098 mm for 7301 and 2.779/1.369/1.964/4.259 mm for 8301. [Script](scratchbook/pointellipse.py), [6301](scratchbook/pointellipse6301.json), [7301](scratchbook/pointellipse7301.json), [8301](scratchbook/pointellipse8301.json), [post-hoc scoring](scratchbook/score_pointellipse.py). This estimator allows common lower-anchor height to vary, unlike the earlier algebraic experiment, which fixed it at the rounded stock value. Thus the two models are not a controlled residual-only comparison. Both are inconsistent with a claim of achieved sub-millimetre accuracy.

A [frozen protocol](scratchbook/protocol.md) specifies a fresh matched stock versus point-specific ellipse comparison with new seed 9301 and uneven friction, on the previously used 7301 layout. Both 9301 runs have completed. It uses known 30 mm radii, zero buildup, 0.06 degree encoder noise, event recording, and normal automatic stopping. The candidate's safety fallback uses only encoder data and fit validity, not truth. The copied harness uses imports from the prior worktree and prior perturbation module, as documented in the earlier report; it is not self-contained.

The fresh stock 9301 world completed normally after 2,335,106 steps and applied A/B/C/D errors of 5.9243/1.6724/0.8104/1.1176 mm. [Firmware, timing and scoring](scratchbook/stock9301-evaluation.json), [run](runs/stock9301/). The [baked audit](scratchbook/stock9301-baked-audit.json) confirms all 13 authored spool/guide translations and SHA-256 `9bc59e465bac72e0e6925b475d499c473b748a61d335a8df55ab7e323ce381c6`. [Native readback](runs/stock9301/sweeps.headless/intervention-native.json) confirms Coulomb/stiction torques A 0.00125/0.002625, B 0.002/0.001125, C 0.0005/0.001875, D 0.00175/0.00075. These are simulation engine torque units, not tension measurements. The matching fresh candidate result follows.

The fresh point-specific candidate completed normally and sent its replacement M669 to firmware. A/B/C/D errors were 4.5360/2.0141/0.7630/3.7967 mm, against stock 5.9243/1.6724/0.8104/1.1176 mm. Maximum error improved, but B and D worsened and three anchors exceeded 1 mm. [Applied command and firmware readback](scratchbook/point9301-evaluation.json), [candidate decision](runs/point9301/point-filter.json), [run](runs/point9301/). The two runs each used 2,335,106 steps and their 160 saved encoder-mean vectors are identical. Their baked scene hashes and all native motor readbacks also match. Thus this comparison holds observations fixed while changing the final fit. The stock quality score shown in the candidate log evaluates the stock command, not the replacement.
